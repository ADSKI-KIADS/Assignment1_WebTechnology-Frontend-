The National Library of the Republic of Kazakhstan

The library's founding date is considered to be December 31, 1910, when a library and reading room was opened by a decision of the Verny City Duma. In 1931, pursuant to the March 12, 1931, decree of the Presidium of the Central Executive Committee of Soviets of the Kazakh ASSR titled "On the Establishment of the State Public Library of the Kazakh ASSR," the institution was reorganized into the State Public Library of the Kazakh SSR. It began serving its first readers on May 5, 1931. In February 1937, the library was named after A.S. Pushkin to commemorate the great Russian poet and mark the centenary of his death. In 1991, under Decree No. 775 of the Cabinet of Ministers of the Kazakh SSR dated December 9, 1991—"On the National Library of the Kazakh SSR"—the A.S. Pushkin State Library of the Kazakh SSR was renamed the National Library of the Kazakh SSR, thereby acquiring state and public significance as an especially valuable cultural asset.

Key dates

  1. 1910 — the year the library opened, following a decision by the Verny City Duma to establish a library and reading room.
  2. 1931 — The library is reorganized into the State Public Library of the Kazakh SSR.
  3. 1937 — The library is named after A.S. Pushkin.
  4. 1991 — The library is renamed the National Library of the Kazakh SSR.

Facts

  • The library building was constructed in 1970 and has been listed as a national historical and cultural monument since 1982.
  • The library has a collection of over 7 million items, including books, journals, and manuscripts.
  • The library is a member of the International Federation of Library Associations and Institutions (IFLA).
  • It serves as the largest scientific and methodological center for libraries across Kazakhstan.

The library remains dedicated to preserving national memory and expanding public access to knowledge.

— National Library of the Republic of Kazakhstan, mission statement
